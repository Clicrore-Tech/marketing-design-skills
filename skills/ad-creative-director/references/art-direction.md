# Art direction

## Priorities, in order

1. **Meaning** - what one thing must the viewer get?
2. **Idea** - the visual metaphor or situation that carries it
3. **Composition** - the big shapes and where the eye lands first
4. **Hierarchy** - first, second, third read
5. **Light, colour, type** - in service of the above
6. **Finish** - texture, detail, polish

Finish never rescues a weak idea.

## Concept toolkit (use different levers for the three concepts)

| Lever | Example for a bakery launch ad |
| --- | --- |
| Metaphor | A loaf rising like a sunrise over the city skyline |
| Emotional stance | Calm craft vs urgent "first batch only" vs proud celebration |
| Person and text | Baker pointing at the headline vs customer mid-bite vs no person |
| Point of view | Over the counter vs from inside the oven vs top-down on the table |
| Story | Before and after of the dough vs one frozen moment |
| Scale | One giant object vs many small ones |

## Moves that reliably work

- **The real expert as hero.** A confident, well-lit portrait of the founder or a staff member with a one-line promise. People trust people more than logos.
- **The result as a physical object.** The finished product, the report, the medal, shot large.
- **The customer's question as the headline.** "Will it fit my budget?" with the answer in the subline.
- **The place at a specific hour.** "4 am, the first batch" beats a generic wide shot.
- **Local anchor.** A recognisable city cue used lightly for local pride.
- **Countdown.** A big date with a real deadline.

## Moves to avoid

- Stock people pointing at nothing
- Collages of 20 photos with no focal point
- Rainbow gradients and glow on every element
- Five fonts in one headline
- Faces cut by text

## YouTube thumbnails

- One face with a clear expression, one object and three to five words
- The words complete the title, they do not repeat it
- Contrast between face and background so it reads at 160px wide
- Look at the top 10 results for that search and break their pattern on purpose
