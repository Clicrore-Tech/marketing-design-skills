# Season calendar, audiences and claims

## Build the brand's own calendar first

Every business has a demand curve. Find it before planning content. Ask for, or read from insights and sales data:

1. **Peak months** - when do most enquiries or sales come in?
2. **Decision window** - how long between first seeing the brand and buying? (A cafe: minutes. A wedding venue: months.)
3. **Fixed dates** - launches, events, results, renewals, festivals the audience celebrates.
4. **Quiet months** - when to build trust and audience instead of selling hard.

Then map content to the curve:

| Phase | Audience mood | Content that works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the decision window | Curious, not ready | Education, behind the scenes, founder and team stories |
| Decision window opens | Comparing options | Proof, reviews, comparisons, price clarity |
| Peak | Ready to act | Offers, slots left (only if true), urgency with a real deadline, lead ads at full budget |
| After purchase | Excited or unsure | Onboarding, customer stories, referral asks |
| Quiet months | Low intent | Community, culture, tips, festival content with a light offer |

## Festival and sales moments (India example)

Adapt to the brand's audience and region: New Year, Republic Day sales, Valentine's week, Holi, Ugadi and Gudi Padwa, Eid, summer holidays, Independence Day sales, Onam, Ganesh Chaturthi, Navratri and Dussehra, Diwali, Christmas and year-end. Festival content should feel native to the festival first and carry the offer second.

## Audience notes

- **The user and the payer** are often different people (children and parents, employees and managers, patients and families). Write for one at a time.
- **Messaging app forwarders** decide a lot of local purchases. A post that makes sense without its caption gets forwarded.
- **People trust faces.** Founder, staff and customer faces beat stock footage for local businesses.

## Claims rules (general advertising practice)

1. Results, ratings and numbers name their source and period.
2. Superlatives like "best" or "No.1" need verifiable third-party evidence shown with the claim.
3. Testimonials are real, current and consented.
4. Show typical results next to exceptional ones.
5. No guarantees you cannot honour.
6. Prices show every mandatory charge.

This is a working summary, not legal advice. When a claim is borderline, flag it and offer compliant wording instead of dropping it silently.

## Meta targeting reminders

- Do not write copy that asserts personal attributes ("You are in debt", "Your bad skin").
- Lead forms convert better with one qualifying question than with a long form.
- Local businesses usually start with a 5 to 15 km radius around the location. Online brands start broad and let the algorithm find buyers.
